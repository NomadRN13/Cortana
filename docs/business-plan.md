# 40/Love — Business Plan

*Love at first serve.* A dating and social app for racquet sports players.

> **A note on numbers.** Market figures in this plan are directional estimates
> from public reporting, included to size the opportunity — verify them against
> current SFIA (Sports & Fitness Industry Association) participation reports,
> USA Pickleball / Association of Pickleball Professionals data, and dating-app
> industry reports before putting them in front of investors.

---

## 1. Executive summary

40/Love connects active singles through the racquet sports they already love,
making the first date a game instead of dinner. Members build a player profile
(sports, skill, availability), match with nearby players, and propose a court
time right in the chat.

Three modes widen the funnel far beyond dating: **Date** (romantic matches),
**Play** (hitting partners), and **Friends** (leagues and social play). Play
and Friends give the app immediate value on day one in a new city — solving
the cold-start problem that kills most niche dating apps — and keep members
engaged after they couple up.

The plan: validate with 50–100 player interviews and a waitlist landing page,
build community before code, launch a lean MVP in a single city with a strong
racquet culture, and expand city by city. Revenue starts with premium
memberships, then event tickets and club partnerships.

## 2. Problem & insight

- **Dating apps optimize for chat, not chemistry.** Endless messaging, few
  meetings, first dates that feel like interviews. App fatigue is widely
  reported, especially among people over 35.
- **Active people want first dates that involve doing something.** A shared
  activity removes the pressure, fills the silences, and shows character —
  how someone competes, loses, and laughs — faster than any bio.
- **Racquet sports come with built-in first-date infrastructure.** Public
  courts, clubs, open plays, and mixers already exist everywhere. No other
  hobby hands you a safe, public, hour-long, naturally-scheduled first date.
- **The pickleball boom is a demographic gift.** It is the fastest-growing
  sport in the US (directional: ~14M players), it skews social by design
  (rotating doubles, open play), and it spans ages 25–75 — including the
  40+ singles that mainstream apps serve worst.

## 3. Solution & positioning

**One-liner:** 40/Love connects active singles through the racquet sports they
already love, making the first date a game instead of dinner.

The core loop: player profile → match on sport, skill, and vibe → propose a
court time in chat → meet and play → "same time next week?"

**Why three modes matter (this is the strategic heart of the product):**

1. **Cold start.** A dating app with 40 users in a city is useless; a
   find-a-hitting-partner app with 40 users is already useful. Play mode makes
   the app worth downloading before dating liquidity exists.
2. **Retention.** Dating apps lose their best users when they succeed. Play
   and Friends modes keep couples, marrieds, and the uninterested in the app —
   maintaining the community that Date mode draws from.
3. **Word of mouth.** "It's how I found my doubles partner" is an easier
   recommendation to make out loud than "it's my dating app."

## 4. Market

| Layer | Directional size | Source to verify |
|---|---|---|
| US tennis players | ~24M | SFIA / USTA participation reports |
| US pickleball players | ~14M, fastest-growing US sport | SFIA, APP/USA Pickleball |
| Padel / squash / racquetball | Several million combined; padel growing fast | SFIA, USPA |
| US online-dating market | Multi-billion-dollar annual revenue; tens of millions of users | Match Group filings, industry reports |

**Serviceable niche:** singles (plus Play/Friends users of any status) inside
racquet communities, addressed one metro at a time. A single healthy launch
city needs only a few thousand active members to feel alive — this is a
beachhead strategy, not a national land-grab.

## 5. Target users

- **"Rally-first" Priya, 29** — urban professional, NTRP 3.5 tennis + casual
  pickleball. Burned out on swipe apps; would absolutely meet someone for an
  hour of hitting. Uses Date + Play.
- **"Kitchen-line" Sam, 45** — divorced, competitive pickleball (DUPR 4.0+),
  plays four mornings a week. Wants to meet someone who shares the obsession;
  hates bar first-dates. Uses Date, stays for Friends events.
- **"New-in-town" Elena, 52** — recently relocated, intermediate pickleball.
  Primary need is community; open to dating if it happens. Enters through
  Friends mode — and is exactly the member who makes Date mode work for others.

## 6. Competition

| Player | What they are | Gap 40/Love fills |
|---|---|---|
| Tinder / Hinge / Bumble | Massive generic dating pools | No shared activity, no skill matching, no "first date = game" mechanic; niche intent gets lost |
| Fitafy and fitness-dating apps | Dating for gym/fitness people | Fitness-broad, not racquet-specific; no court scheduling, no events, thin US liquidity |
| Playtomic, PicklePlay, court apps | Booking and partner-finding utilities | Explicitly not dating; no romantic intent layer, no profiles built for chemistry |
| Meetup / club leagues | Offline social infrastructure | No matching, no intent signaling; 40/Love partners with these rather than competing |

**Positioning:** on a 2×2 of *generic ↔ racquet-specific* and *dating ↔ play*,
the dating × racquet-specific quadrant is empty. That's the lane.

## 7. Validation plan (Phase 1 — weeks 1–4)

Interview 50–100 racquet players before writing code.

**Questions** (the founder's four, plus three):
1. Would you use a dating app specifically for racquet sports?
2. What's frustrating about meeting other players?
3. Would you rather meet through a game than a traditional date?
4. What features would make you download it?
5. Are you single, and does that change your answer? *(separates Date-mode demand from Play-mode demand)*
6. What would make you feel safe meeting a stranger for a first game?
7. Would you pay for anything here — and what?

**Where to find interviewees:** local courts and clubs, pickleball open plays,
league nights, r/pickleball and r/10s, city pickleball/tennis Facebook groups,
Meetup racquet groups.

**Success criteria (decide before you start):**
- ≥40% of singles interviewed say they would try it
- ≥60% say they'd rather meet through a game than a standard first date
- ≥15% of visitors to the landing page join the waitlist
- ≥1 club or organizer willing to co-host a "40/Love Social Mixer"

If the numbers land: proceed to MVP. If they miss: the interviews will say
whether the fix is positioning (lead with Play mode?) or premise.

## 8. Go-to-market

**Community before code:**
- Instagram + TikTok (@fortylove): racquet culture content, dating-fatigue
  humor, court-date ideas, local player spotlights.
- Partner with clubs and organizers to co-host **40/Love Social Mixers** —
  round robins engineered for meeting people. The mixer is the product demo.
- Waitlist landing page (built — `landing/index.html`) collecting emails and
  city votes.

**City-first launch — city #1 is Indianapolis.** Chosen on the launch
criteria (courts per capita, pickleball scene strength, singles density,
weather, founder community roots — the last mattering more than any
statistic): Indy Parks operates 65+ public pickleball courts, the metro has
at least five dedicated indoor pickleball facilities (one open 24/7) with
The Picklr and Chicken N Pickle both investing locally, and Greater
Indianapolis is one of the Midwest's largest tennis markets (50+ dedicated
indoor courts, active USTA Central Indiana section with social formats like
brewery-based "Tennis on Tap"). Indoor density de-risks the Midwest winter.
Full scorecard and ranked partner targets: `outreach/indianapolis/targets.md`.
Note: Indiana currently has no padel facilities — Indy launch copy leads
with pickleball and tennis.

**90-day city playbook:** 2 mixers/month from week 1 → app invite-only to
mixer attendees and waitlist (scarcity + safety) → open the app when Discover
feels alive (roughly 500+ active members) → local club/coach partnerships for
events and promotion.

**Expansion trigger:** expand to city #2 only when city #1 hits sustained
marks — e.g. 1,000+ weekly actives, week-4 retention ≥30%, and events selling
out — so the playbook is proven, not just survived.

## 9. Business model

Freemium core: joining, matching, messaging, and event browsing stay free —
liquidity is the product; never tax it early.

**Sequenced revenue:**
1. **Premium membership** (from v2.0): see who liked you, unlimited likes,
   profile boosts, advanced filters (skill band, availability windows),
   travel mode. Anchor: $12–15/month.
2. **Events** (as soon as demand exists): ticketed mixers and round robins;
   club partnership packages (featured events, member funnels).
3. **Later:** coaching promotions, tournament sponsorships, equipment
   affiliates — only once the community is big enough that these feel like
   perks, not ads.

**Illustrative unit sketch** *(directional, for planning only)*: 10,000 members
in city #1 × 5% premium × $13/mo ≈ **$78k ARR** from subscriptions, before
events. Two ticketed mixers/month at 40 tickets × $15 adds ≈ $14k/yr per city.
Real numbers will differ; the point is that a single healthy city plausibly
funds its own operations.

## 10. Build options & recommendation

| Option | Cost (directional) | Speed | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| No-code (Bubble, FlutterFlow, Glide) | $5–15k or founder sweat | 4–8 weeks | Fastest validation; platform limits and migration debt later |
| Freelance developers | $25–60k | 3–5 months | Custom UX; needs founder product management |
| Agency | $80–200k+ | 4–6+ months | Polish; burns runway before validation |

**Recommendation:** landing page + mixers + interviews **now** (cost ≈ $0);
if validation passes, a no-code or lean-freelance MVP scoped to the roadmap's
v0.1. Do not engage an agency before the concept is validated in one city.
Plan the no-code exit: keep the brand, data export, and domain portable.

## 12. Risks & mitigations

- **Liquidity / cold start** — *the* niche-dating killer. Mitigate: one city,
  invite-only start, Play/Friends modes for day-one value, mixers as the
  offline flywheel.
- **Safety & trust** — meeting strangers is the product. Mitigate: photo
  verification, in-app report/block from v1.1, defaulting first meets to
  public courts and daytime slots, event-first meeting options, clear
  community guidelines.
- **Seasonality** — outdoor sports dip in winter, and Indianapolis has real
  winters. Mitigate: Indy's unusually deep indoor inventory (24/7 pickleball
  clubs, 50+ indoor tennis courts) via club partnerships, and indoor event
  programming November–March.
- **Success churn** — couples leave dating apps. Mitigate: Play/Friends modes
  are the retention layer; "met on 40/Love" stories are marketing gold, not
  losses.
- **Copycats** — a Match Group experiment could clone the concept. Mitigate:
  own the offline layer (mixers, club relationships) that platforms won't
  build city by city; move fast in the niche.
- **Platform costs** — App Store fees (15–30%) compress subscription margin.
  Mitigate: web signup/billing where policy allows; events revenue is
  fee-free.

## 13. KPIs from day one

- Waitlist signups per week (and per city)
- Interview "would try it" rate
- Mixer attendance and repeat-attendance rate
- Activation: % of signups completing a profile with sports + skill
- **Weekly court-dates proposed per active member** — the north-star metric
- Message response rate; week-4 retention; Play-vs-Date mode usage split
