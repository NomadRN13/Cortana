## Slide 1 — Title

> # 40/Love
> ### *Love at first serve.*
>
> The dating app for racquet sports people — where the first date is a game
> instead of dinner.

**Speaker notes:** Open with the name. "In tennis, 'love' means zero — 40/love
is match point. We think meeting someone should feel like that: a game you're
excited to play, not an interview you have to sit through."

## Slide 2 — Problem

> ## Dating apps are all chat, no chemistry.
> - Endless messaging, few real meetings
> - First dates are interviews over drinks
> - Active people want to *do something*, not audition
> - Over-35s — the fastest-growing singles group — are served worst

**Speaker notes:** Everyone in the room knows app fatigue firsthand or from
friends. The core failure: apps optimize for time-in-app, not great first
meetings. For active people, a table-for-two is the worst possible venue to
show who they actually are.

## Slide 3 — Why now

> ## The racquet boom met the dating slump.
> - Pickleball: fastest-growing US sport, ~14M players *(directional)*
> - ~24M US tennis players; padel arriving fast *(directional)*
> - Courts, clubs, and mixers booming in every metro
> - Activity-first dating is a rising behavior with no category owner

**Speaker notes:** Two curves crossing: racquet participation exploding —
across every age group — while dating-app satisfaction falls. Pickleball
especially is *engineered* for meeting people: rotating doubles, open play.
The infrastructure for activity-dates already exists; nobody has put a dating
layer on it.

## Slide 4 — Solution

> ## The first date is a game.
> - Match on **sport, skill, and vibe** — not just photos
> - Propose a **court time** right in the chat
> - Three modes: ❤️ **Date** · 🎾 **Play** · 👥 **Friends**

**Speaker notes:** 40/Love matches people who play the same sport at a
compatible level nearby, then turns the awkward "so… drinks?" into a one-tap
court-time proposal. And it's more than dating: Play mode finds hitting
partners, Friends mode covers leagues and socials. That's strategic, not
decorative — next slide shows why.

## Slide 5 — Product

> ## Profile → Match → Court time → Play.
> - Player profile: sports, skill (NTRP/DUPR), availability
> - Discover: like, pass, or **propose a match**
> - "It's a Match Point!" → chat → structured court-time card
> - Events: mixers and round robins, in-app
>
> *Live clickable prototype: `app/index.html`*

**Speaker notes:** Walk the prototype here — welcome, profile, Discover swipe,
the court-time proposal card in chat, events. Point out the "your serve" reply
nudge: the app's language keeps pushing toward meeting, not endless chatting.

## Slide 6 — Market

> ## Beachhead, not land-grab. *(all figures directional)*
> - **TAM:** 40M+ US racquet-sport players
> - **SAM:** active singles in racquet communities — millions
> - **SOM:** launch city (Indianapolis): 5–10k members in 18 months
> - US online dating: a multi-billion-dollar annual market

**Speaker notes:** We size honestly: the wedge is one city's racquet community.
A few thousand active local members makes the app feel alive — that's the
entire near-term goal, and the model that then repeats city by city. Play and
Friends modes mean the addressable pool is every player, not only singles.

## Slide 7 — Business model

> ## Free to play. Premium to win.
> 1. **Premium** — $12–15/mo: see likes, boosts, filters *(first)*
> 2. **Events & clubs** — ticketed mixers, club partnerships *(second)*
> 3. **Later** — coaching promos, sponsorships, gear affiliates
>
> Matching and messaging stay free — liquidity is the product.

**Speaker notes:** Sequenced deliberately: monetizing a thin network kills it,
so premium waits for retention proof. Events monetize early without taxing
matching — people happily pay $15 for a great round robin. One healthy city
plausibly covers its own operating costs; the upside is the repeatable
playbook.

## Slide 8 — Go-to-market

> ## Community before code. One city at a time.
> - **City #1: Indianapolis** — 65+ public pickleball courts, deep indoor scene
> - @fortylove content: racquet culture × dating humor
> - Club partnerships + **40/Love Social Mixers**
> - Waitlist with city voting → invite-only soft launch
> - Expand to city #2 only on proven retention marks

**Speaker notes:** The mixer is the demo: we host round robins built for
meeting people, and the app is the take-home. Invite-only launch keeps early
density high and creepiness low. Indianapolis wins on the criteria — courts
per capita, one of the Midwest's strongest pickleball scenes, big indoor
inventory for winter — plus our real community roots there, which matter more
than any statistic. The scouted partner list (clubs, USTA Central Indiana,
Indy Parks) is already in the repo.

## Slide 9 — Competition

> ## An empty quadrant.
>
> |  | Play-focused | Dating-focused |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Generic** | Meetup, gym apps | Tinder, Hinge, Bumble |
> | **Racquet-specific** | Playtomic, PicklePlay | **40/Love** |

**Speaker notes:** Draw the 2×2: horizontal axis play↔dating, vertical
generic↔racquet-specific. Mainstream apps have scale but no activity mechanic;
court utilities have players but explicitly no romance. The dating ×
racquet-specific quadrant is empty. Our moat isn't code — it's the offline
layer: mixers, club relationships, city density that a Match Group experiment
won't grind out city by city.
